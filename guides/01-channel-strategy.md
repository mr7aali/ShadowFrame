## Core format decisions

| Decision | Current choice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Original fictional crime thrillers | Never presented as real news or true crime |
| Episode length | **14 minutes** (~2,100–2,200 narrated words) | Change here and in [03-story-and-script.md](03-story-and-script.md) if you switch |
| Delivery | **Narration-led**, with short dialogue lines only where they add impact | Keeps lip-sync shots to a minimum (cheaper, more reliable) |

## Channel concept
- [ ] Write a one-sentence channel promise: what viewers will consistently get.
- [ ] Choose the main subgenre: detective mystery, psychological thriller, serial-killer investigation, courtroom mystery, heist, or supernatural crime.
- [ ] Decide whether stories are standalone or part of a connected series.
- [ ] Choose a tone: grounded realistic, dark cinematic, fast-paced, investigative, or psychological.
- [ ] Set a content boundary: fictional stories only; do not present invented events as real news or true crime.
- [ ] Define the channel's unique selling point (recurring detective, twist endings, detailed clues, moral dilemmas).
- [ ] Choose a realistic publishing schedule.
- [ ] Define how Shorts will support long-form episodes.

## Target audience: USA and English-speaking countries

Main viewers are in the United States; the UK, Canada, and Australia are secondary markets.

- **Language:** Plain, conversational American English. American spelling (color, neighborhood). Avoid heavy slang or regional idioms.
- **Settings:** Familiar American places — a small Midwest town, a rainy Pacific Northwest city, a Southern port, a New York precinct. Fictional town names avoid factual errors.
- **Details that must feel right:** Police ranks and procedures, 911 calls, sheriff vs. city police, dollars and miles, the 12-hour clock.
- **Age and taste:** Core audience is adults roughly 25–54. Mature tone, but no graphic gore (it limits monetization and reach).
- **Monetization note:** U.S., UK, Canadian, and Australian viewers typically bring higher ad rates. Rates vary — check your own Analytics.
