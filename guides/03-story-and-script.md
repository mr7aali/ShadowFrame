# 03 — Story and Script

Related prompts: [script pipeline](../prompts/script/) — idea → outline → script → editor → visual notes → shorts

## Length

Narration runs about 150 words per minute. A 14-minute episode needs **~2,100–2,200 words** (a little less if you add long pauses or music-only moments).

## 14-minute structure

| Time | Part | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Hook — a shocking moment or question, starting mid-action | ~75 |
| 0:30–3:00 | Setup — protagonist, setting, stakes, the crime | ~375 |
| 3:00–7:00 | Investigation — clues, suspects, red herrings | ~600 |
| 7:00–10:00 | Complication — a twist, a death, a betrayal | ~450 |
| 10:00–13:00 | Climax and reveal | ~450 |
| 13:00–14:00 | Ending — consequence plus a chilling last line | ~150 |
| | **Total** | **~2,100** |

Adjust timings to the story; never stretch a short story just to hit a duration.

## Avoid a repetitive formula

YouTube can limit monetization for mass-produced, near-identical videos. Rotate the story shape between episodes:

- Classic whodunit (clues + red herring + twist)
- Locked-room / impossible crime
- Heist gone wrong
- Unreliable narrator
- Told in reverse (start with the confession)
- Race against time (the next victim is known)
- The detective is a suspect

## Story concept checklist
- [ ] One-line premise (logline).
- [ ] Central mystery/crime and emotional stakes.
- [ ] Who the audience follows and why they care.
- [ ] Beginning, escalation, reveal, climax, resolution.
- [ ] Clues planted before the reveal; twist fair in hindsight.
- [ ] Culprit's motive and actions make sense.
- [ ] No plot holes, impossible timelines, or unexplained character knowledge.
- [ ] Original — not a rewrite of a film, TV episode, book, game, or another creator's script.
- [ ] Setting details feel right for U.S. viewers (see [01-channel-strategy.md](01-channel-strategy.md)).

## Writing style
- Short, punchy sentences for tension; no long descriptions.
- A cliffhanger every 2–3 minutes.
- Section markers like `[0:00-0:30]` and `[VISUAL: ...]` notes for scenes.
- Plain American English.

## Script quality control
- [ ] Read the script aloud and fix unnatural lines.
- [ ] Remove repetitive exposition and unnecessary narration.
- [ ] Every scene changes the plot, reveals character, or builds tension.
- [ ] Each speaker has a distinct voice and vocabulary.
- [ ] Check spelling, grammar, names, dates, locations.
- [ ] The ending pays off the clues.
- [ ] Word count is within 2,000–2,300.
- [ ] Human edit pass: add your own voice and style — do not publish raw AI output.
- [ ] Lock a final approved version; never generate visuals or voice from a draft.
