## [0:00–0:30] HOOK

[SHOT 01] Every clock in Walter Krane's shop was still ticking when they found him.

[SHOT 02] Forty-one clocks. Grandfather clocks. Cuckoo clocks. Railroad clocks that hadn't lost a second in a hundred years.

[SHOT 03] Only one had stopped. The gold pocket watch in Walter's vest. Its glass was shattered. Its hands were frozen at 9:14.

[SHOT 04] Everyone in Pine Hollow agreed. That was the minute Walter Krane died.

[SHOT 05] Everyone except Detective Mara Quinn. Because a watchmaker's watch doesn't lie. But somebody in Pine Hollow did.

## [0:30–3:00] SETUP

[SHOT 06] Pine Hollow, Minnesota. January, 1996. Population two thousand and eleven. One stoplight. One diner. And one sheriff's detective who swore she would never come back.

[SHOT 07] Mara Quinn grew up here. Her father, Frank Quinn, was county sheriff for twenty years. He was the reason she became a cop. He was also the reason she left.

[SHOT 08] Now she was back, working nights, with a thin scar through her left eyebrow and a habit of trusting her first instinct. It was usually right. Usually.

[SHOT 09] The call came in at 9:41 p.m., in the middle of the worst blizzard in a decade.

[SHOT 10] A young woman's voice, shaking. "It's Mr. Krane. He's at the bottom of the stairs. He's not moving."

[SHOT 11] Mara drove down Main Street at fifteen miles an hour, wipers fighting the snow. Deputy Eli Booker sat beside her, pen already out, notebook on his knee. Eli wrote everything down. Mara found it annoying. She also found it useful.

[SHOT 12] Krane Clock and Watch Repair sat across the street from the Lamplight Diner. The shop lights were on. The front door was unlocked.

[SHOT 13] Mara stopped on the steps. In the fresh snow were two lines of small boot prints, both leading to the door. One line was sharp and clean. The other was soft, half filled with new snow. She noticed it. Then the ticking pulled her inside.

[SHOT 14] The woman who called was Tess Harlan, twenty-six, Walter's apprentice. Auburn hair. A red wool scarf. A brass jeweler's loupe on a cord around her neck. Her eyes were red from crying.

[SHOT 15] "I came back for my scarf," she said. "I left it here when we closed at six. The lights were on. I called his name. Then I saw him."

[SHOT 16] Walter Krane lay at the bottom of the steep wooden stairs that led down to his workshop. Sixty-eight years old. His wire glasses lay cracked beside him.

[SHOT 17] Eli crouched down. "Ice on his boots. Steep stairs. Looks like he slipped, Detective."

[SHOT 18] Mara knelt and lifted the gold pocket watch from Walter's vest. Glass broken. Hands frozen at 9:14. And the little winding crown on top was pulled all the way out.

[SHOT 19] She frowned at it for a long moment. Then she slipped it into an evidence bag. "Maybe," she said. "Find out where everyone was at 9:14."

## [3:00–7:00] INVESTIGATION

[SHOT 20] Pine Hollow had no secrets. Only things people hadn't said yet. By midnight, Mara had heard three of them.

[SHOT 21] The first came from Joyce Abbott, who had run the Lamplight Diner for thirty years. Grey curls. Reading glasses on a beaded chain. She poured Mara a coffee without asking.

[SHOT 22] "Walter's nephew was here tonight," Joyce said. "Ronnie. Around eight, he stormed across the street and into the shop. I could hear the shouting through the snow. Ten minutes later he came out and peeled off in that truck of his."

[SHOT 23] "Shouting about what?"
"Money. With Ronnie, it's always money."
Behind the register, Mara noticed a brass key on a hook, tagged with a little paper label that said "Krane." "You have a key to his shop?" "Thirty years," Joyce said. "In case of fire." She didn't mention that Walter had refused, twice, to sell her his building so she could double the size of her diner. Eli found that out later, from the county clerk.

[SHOT 24] The second secret came from Walter's lawyer, woken by a phone call at half past midnight. Walter's will was simple. The shop, the building, and every clock inside it went to his only family. Ronnie Krane.

[SHOT 25] And the third secret came from Ronnie himself.

[SHOT 26] They found him at two in the morning, in a trailer off Route 9, still wearing his denim jacket with the sheepskin collar. Stocky. Unshaven. Eyes that wouldn't settle on anything.

[SHOT 27] "Yeah, I asked him for a loan," Ronnie said. "I owe some people. He said no. I left. He was fine."
"Where were you at 9:14?"
"Here. Asleep."

[SHOT 28] Eli walked out to Ronnie's pickup and laid a glove on the hood. Snow was melting off it. The engine was still warm. Ronnie hadn't been asleep. Not even close.

[SHOT 29] Mara felt the case click into place. A debt. An inheritance. An argument. A lie. Her first instinct said Ronnie Krane. And her first instinct was usually right.

[SHOT 30] But at dawn, back at the shop, something kept pulling at her.

[SHOT 31] Tess was there, sweeping up broken glass with shaking hands. She had refused to go home. "Someone should wind them," she said quietly, looking up at the clocks. "He wound every one of them himself. Every night at nine." She smiled weakly. "He used to say a good watchmaker can hear a lie. A watch that runs too perfectly is hiding something."

[SHOT 32] Mara held up the evidence bag. "His pocket watch. The crown's pulled out. Why would it be like that?"

[SHOT 33] Tess barely glanced at it. "The fall," she said. "It must have caught on something." Then she went back to sweeping.

[SHOT 34] Back at the diner, Mara asked Joyce one more question. "Was Tess Harlan in here last night?"

[SHOT 35] Joyce nodded. "Came in a little before nine. Sat right there at the counter. Red in the face, like she'd been running. Ordered a coffee she never touched." Joyce paused. "And she kept asking me what time it was. Three, maybe four times. Then she left around nine-thirty."

[SHOT 36] "Did that seem strange to you?"
Joyce shrugged. "Young people. Always somewhere to be."
And Joyce herself? She had served eleven customers between eight and ten. Every one of them remembered her refilling their coffee. Not one of them saw her leave the counter. Whatever Joyce wanted from Walter's building, she hadn't crossed that street to get it.

[SHOT 37] So at 9:14, Tess Harlan had been sitting in front of the one witness everybody in town trusted. The perfect alibi. Mara wrote that down. Then she underlined the word "perfect."

## [7:00–10:00] COMPLICATION

[SHOT 38] By noon, the sheriff had made his decision. Ronnie Krane was arrested on suspicion of murder. The whole town exhaled. People stopped her at the gas station to shake her hand. The nephew. Of course it was the nephew. Everybody had always said Ronnie would end up like this.

[SHOT 39] Then Eli walked into Mara's office and dropped his notebook on her desk. "Ronnie lied," he said. "Just not about what we thought. At 9:14 he was in the basement of the Moose Creek Sportsmen's Club, losing two hundred dollars at a card game he's not supposed to be at. Seven men saw him. Two of them are volunteer firemen."

[SHOT 40] Mara stared at the notebook. Her first instinct had been wrong. And if Ronnie didn't do it, then someone in Pine Hollow had watched her chase the wrong man. And felt relieved.
Her father used to say it at the dinner table, tapping his fork on his plate. "The first answer, Mara, is usually the one somebody wants you to find." She had hated it when she was sixteen. She hated it more now, because he was right.

[SHOT 41] She went back to the shop alone.

[SHOT 42] In daylight, the workshop looked smaller. Rows of tiny drawers. Tweezers. Springs no bigger than an eyelash. On the bench lay Walter's repair ledger, a thick green book where he logged every job in neat, heavy pencil.

[SHOT 43] She turned to the last entry. It was dated three days ago. Then she saw the stub. A page had been torn out. Cleanly. Carefully. By someone who didn't want to leave a ragged edge.

[SHOT 44] Behind her, a floorboard creaked. Tess stood at the top of the stairs.

[SHOT 45] "I wanted to tell you," Tess said. "Something's missing. An antique railroad watch. The Bergstrom family brought it in last week. It's worth thousands." She swallowed. "Ronnie knew about it. Walter showed him."

[SHOT 46] It was helpful. It was exactly what a grieving apprentice would say. And it pointed straight back at the nephew who now had seven witnesses. Twice now, every road in this case had led to Ronnie. Roads don't do that on their own. Somebody builds them.

[SHOT 47] Mara looked at the young woman on the stairs. Then she looked down at the snow melting off Tess's black boots. Small boots. A narrow, zigzag tread. The same tread as both lines of footprints outside the door.

[SHOT 48] One line, sharp and fresh, from when Tess arrived at 9:38. And one line, soft and half buried, from someone who had walked to that door much earlier in the storm.

[SHOT 49] Tess had told her she came back once. The snow said she came back twice.

## [10:00–13:00] CLIMAX AND REVEAL

[SHOT 50] The next night, at nine o'clock, Mara asked Tess to meet her at the shop. "Help me wind the clocks," she said. "Walter would have wanted it."

[SHOT 51] They worked in silence. Forty-one clocks. Tess's hands were steady now. Quick. Practiced. A real watchmaker's hands. Outside, the snow had stopped. Across the street, the lights of the Lamplight Diner glowed. Joyce was watching through the window. Mara had asked her to.

[SHOT 52] When the last clock began to tick, Mara set the green ledger on the bench. She had rubbed a soft pencil across the blank page beneath the torn one. Walter always pressed hard. His last words had come through in pale grey letters.

[SHOT 53] She read them aloud. "Bergstrom railroad watch. Movement not original. Replaced with a cheap copy. Fourth one this year. All handled by T.H. Call sheriff in the morning."

[SHOT 54] Tess didn't move. Then she let out a small, broken laugh. "Anyone could write T.H.," she said. "Anyone could walk through snow. You arrested the wrong man once already, Detective. Are you sure you want to do it twice?"

[SHOT 55] "You were swapping the movements," Mara said. "The real antique works out. Cheap copies in. Customers never open the back. But Walter did. He found out last night and called you back to the shop to explain. That's the first set of footprints. Around a quarter to nine."

[SHOT 56] "You argued at the top of those stairs. He fell. Maybe you pushed him. Maybe you didn't. But then you did something only a watchmaker would think to do."

[SHOT 57] Mara held up the bagged pocket watch. "You pulled out the crown. On a watch like this, that stops the hands. You set them to 9:14, cracked the glass, and put it back in his vest. Then you walked across the street and asked Joyce the time, again and again, until she would never forget you were there."

[SHOT 58] "A fall doesn't pull a crown out, Tess. Fingers do. And this morning, the state lab found a print on that crown. A small one. Not Walter's."

[SHOT 59] For a long moment, there was only ticking. Forty-one clocks, all keeping perfect time.

[SHOT 60] "My mother's care home costs nineteen hundred dollars a month," Tess whispered. "I was going to put them all back. I swear I was going to put them back." Her voice cracked. "He wouldn't listen. He just kept saying 'sheriff.' I only grabbed his arm."

[SHOT 61] Eli stepped out of the shadows by the door, where he had been standing the whole time. Tess Harlan held out her wrists before he even asked.

## [13:00–14:00] ENDING

[SHOT 62] Ronnie Krane was released the next morning. He inherited forty-one clocks and sold every one of them within a month. Tess Harlan pleaded guilty to manslaughter and theft. In court, she never once looked up at the clock on the wall.

[SHOT 63] In Tess's apartment, deputies found a shoebox under the bed. Inside were the stolen antique movements, each wrapped in tissue paper and labeled in her careful handwriting.

[SHOT 64] Mara went through them one by one. Bergstrom. Lindqvist. Halvorsen. Then she stopped.

[SHOT 65] The last one still had its original silver case back. Engraved on it were four words. "For Frank. Never late."

[SHOT 66] It was her father's watch. Frank Quinn had been dead for twenty years. And Mara had watched them bury him with that watch on his wrist.
